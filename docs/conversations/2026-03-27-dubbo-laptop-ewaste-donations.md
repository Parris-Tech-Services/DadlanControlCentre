## Assistant

A strong angle here is: **donation is easier when you target organisations with an actual disposal pathway, community-benefit logic, and someone who can approve exceptions**. From the public material I found, **Charles Sturt is the clearest lead**: it has a formal IT equipment disposal policy, an e-waste disposal process, and a published report stating it can donate obsolete IT equipment to charities and not-for-profits on request. By contrast, **Dubbo Regional Council publicly points computers to e-waste recycling depots / Officeworks rather than a donation channel**. Also, organisations handling sensitive data have real disposal friction, because IT equipment with media must be sanitised first, and if it cannot be sanitised it should be destroyed. A current NSW Police disposal policy is useful as a model too: it explicitly includes donation / concessional disposal to charities, community, and non-profits, but wraps that in fairness, approval, value, and record-keeping requirements.

Source used in the discussion: https://policy.csu.edu.au/document/view-current.php?id=399&version=1
