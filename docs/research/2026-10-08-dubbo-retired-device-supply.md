# Dubbo Retired-Device Supply — 2026 Evidence Update

**Date:** 8 October 2026  
**Purpose:** update DadLAN's March 2026 laptop-donation research with the strongest new quantitative evidence found during the Dubbo circular-economy deep-research pass.

Canonical wider research:
https://github.com/joshualparris/DubboEwaste/blob/main/research/circular-economy/14-deep-research-10-biggest-gaps-2026-10-08.md

## Bottom line

We still do **not** have a defensible number for how many usable laptops are retired in Dubbo each year.

However, the evidence base is materially better:

1. NSW Government's 12-month Device Bank procurement expected about **2,800 surplus laptops already in storage** plus about **2,500 more during the contract term** — roughly **5,300 laptops statewide**.
2. Those devices were described as predominantly HP x360 1030 G3/G4, with some G7/G8, and mostly five years old or older.
3. NSW Education publicly acknowledged a **backlog of schools requesting eWaste collection** and says it is working through the backlog with an unnamed vendor.
4. The Department has a formal e-waste disposal process; public material still does not provide Dubbo/Western NSW quantities or name the current vendor.
5. Historical Charles Sturt evidence proves donation of obsolete IT to charities/NFPs has occurred, but the old donation counts are not evidence of current Dubbo supply.

## What this changes for DadLAN

The question is no longer “do NSW institutions retire laptop fleets?” They clearly do.

The unanswered question is:

> **What share of those devices can realistically be accessed in Dubbo under current disposal, probity, sanitisation and community-benefit rules?**

Do not estimate a Dubbo share by population. Public-sector device distribution is driven by agency location, refresh schedules, contracts and central asset processes.

## What would actually answer the Dubbo volume question

For each major local institution, request the following for FY2024/25 and FY2025/26:

- active laptop fleet size;
- active desktop fleet size;
- normal refresh age/cycle;
- number retired;
- make/model/age distribution;
- number redeployed internally;
- number donated;
- number sold/remarketed;
- number recycled;
- number destroyed;
- current disposal/recovery provider;
- next expected batch;
- whether a community or NFP transfer is permitted.

Highest-value targets remain:

- NSW Education / Dubbo public schools;
- TAFE NSW;
- Charles Sturt University;
- Western NSW Local Health District / eHealth NSW;
- Dubbo Regional Council;
- larger non-government schools;
- large Dubbo private employers.

## DadLAN-specific interpretation

For DadLAN, the best target is not “10 random e-waste laptops”.

Prefer:
- known-authority devices;
- matching or near-matching business-class models;
- Windows 11-capable where possible;
- chargers included;
- no unresolved activation/account locks;
- battery condition known;
- devices transferred before they disappear into a material-recycling contract.

A 10-device donation is operationally tiny compared with statewide retirement volumes. The difficulty is **governance and access**, not proof that surplus devices exist.
